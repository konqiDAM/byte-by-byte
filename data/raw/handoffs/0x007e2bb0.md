## What made the difference (base 8 bytes -> best 4 bytes)

The rival wrote the column product as an inlined member:


Rewriting the call site with an explicit temporary Point and the three rows written out
(`out\0x007e2bb0\c_col-temp.cpp`, the `col-temp` variant of `sweep2.py`) fixed the third row's

## Diagnosis / what to try next

The residue is the x87 **operand-order rank sort** for the first `faddp` only:
`compiler_tips.md` §"Operand order: the rank sort" (an add's operands are sorted by internal
symbol number, highest first, and in x87 the higher becomes the `fld` that starts the pair).
Our two product temporaries come out in the opposite order to the original at exactly one site;
the identical source shape at the *second* and *third* dot products (and all of RowProduct) is
already correct, so the tie is decided by the surrounding block state, not by the source spelling.

Levers not yet exhausted:
1. Move the block boundary: make the original's first dot product the *second* statement computed
   (e.g. compute into two temporaries and assign in x,y,z store order) so the first faddp sits in
   the context where our order is already right.
2. Change the symbol the point is reached through for the **first row only** (a `const Point&`
   that aliases `delta` was worse because it re-numbered all three rows — restrict the alias to
   the first row's expression by computing `t.x` first, then dropping the alias).
3. A `volatile float` read of `delta.x` before the statement (compiler_tips: "a volatile read into
   a named local ... orders the loads after it") — it will add instructions, so it only works if
   the original's `fld` is the expression's first load.
4. The build host's solver on this candidate: it is 4 bytes from a match, all in one region.
