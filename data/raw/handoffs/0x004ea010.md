## Hypotheses for the next agent
1. The original is **one scratch/value more expensive** in the loop's block than anything we produce
   (`compiler_tips.md`: colouring cost = uses x P x block frequency, P = distinct values in the
   block). Both differences are exactly "+1 value" shifts. Find a construct that adds one *value*
   to the loop block at zero byte cost — that is the whole problem. Adding a real one costs bytes:
   count copied to a local -> 137, `if (i >= count) break` -> 139.
2. Suspect the original fetched the entry through something we cannot express directly: a member
   returning `SaveEntry**` inlined, or the `enabled` test spelled in a way that keeps a second
   consumer. The `__thiscall` `this`-hint for `FUN_0049cb90` (entry must be in ecx at 0x53) may be
   what drags the base temp into ecx in the original: `mov ecx,[ebx+0x38]; mov ecx,[ecx+edi*4]` is
   the "load straight into the hinted register" form MSVC emits when the intermediate is dead.
3. Also worth a try: `char`/`short` field types in the stand-ins, `SaveEntry** const entries`,
   or `owner` passed as a reference into an inlined helper (v02 showed `owner` itself can move to
   ebp, so the allocator does react to wider changes — a structural rewrite is not hopeless).
