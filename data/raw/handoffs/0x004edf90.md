## NEW LAW (round 2, measured — this is the important result)

**The two residues are ONE pick, and it happens at 0x25.** The vtable/`call eax` group is a *consequence*
of the casebytes load: in **both** binaries the function-pointer temp reuses the register that held the
switch index, and the vtable temp takes the other free register.

* ORIG: casebytes dest = `eax` (**in-place**, reuses the index register) -> index reg = `eax` -> fn = `eax`, vtbl = `edx`.
* OURS: casebytes dest = `edx` (**not** in-place) -> index reg = `edx` -> fn = `edx`, vtbl = `eax`.

Proof: `out\df90sel\s15_uchaccsub.cpp` (byte-arithmetic selector) produces **ORIG's exact 0x25 line, exact
0x2c line and the exact `mov edx,[ecx] / mov eax,[edx+0x154] / call eax`** group. Its *only* difference is the
selector prologue, 3 bytes instead of 2:

```
ORIG 0019 movzx eax,[ecx+4] / add eax,-0x20            (6 bytes)
YOURS 0019 mov dl,[ecx+4] / sub dl,0x20 / movzx eax,dl  (9 bytes)
```
(the +3 is absorbed by the alignment pad: ORIG has `lea ecx,[ecx]`, ours `nop`; extent stays 100.)

So the whole remaining gap is: **produce `movzx eax,[ecx+4]; add eax,-0x20` (2 instructions, 32-bit, in place)
AND then let the casebytes load reuse `eax` in place.** The 32-bit form fuses the `add` into the selector's
own vreg and the allocator then refuses the in-place reuse at 0x25; the byte form arrives at the switch as a
fresh *copy* vreg (`movzx eax,dl`) and the reuse happens. Find a front-end that gives a 2-instruction selector
**that is a copy**, and the function falls out (the vtable/`call eax` group follows for free — do not chase it
separately, and do not use the `?:` value form: it costs a `mov ecx,eax` fixup and is now unnecessary).
