## The one informative experiment (worth keeping)
Call statement moved **between** the two dword stores (`v2/B_beforeA8.cpp`, ndiff 10) produces:

```
002e fstp [esi+4]
0031 mov ecx, edi      <-- exactly the ORIG slot
0033 mov [esi+0x2c], eax
0036 call FUN_004022f0 <-- but the call follows immediately (ORIG has the 0xc store here)
```

So the scheduler *will* place the receiver copy at 0x31, but only when the call statement itself is
emitted there — the call is never sunk. Under that model the original's receiver copy was emitted
6 slots before its call, which no single-statement spelling reproduces. Any call-placement change
(`B_beforeF0/A7/S9`, `v2`) moves the whole call and raises ndiff to 10–31.

## Next ideas for whoever picks this up
1. Something that emits `mov ecx, edi` (a __thiscall receiver materialised in ecx) **between** the two
   dword stores with *zero other instructions*, so the call at 0x46 reuses that ecx. Inlined accessors
   fold; try an inline `__thiscall` helper whose result is *not* folded (e.g. one that takes/returns the
   pointer through a second parameter), or a `struct`/union copy that forces the receiver into ecx.
2. Add a named alias whose *value* is used by the call so the allocator prefers ecx for it — but
   ecx is call-clobbered, so it must not live across a call.
3. The class-2 lever from the brief (change what produces the value in eax before the store at 0x33)
   is the only other mechanism: here eax comes from the `GetSpellType` vcall at 0x26, which matches.
