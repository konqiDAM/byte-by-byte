## Micro-probe: the allocation order, isolated

`probe.cpp` in the claim folder is the same source truncated just after the bounds block (no triangle
loop, no vertex loop), compiled directly:

```powershell
$env:F3_MSVC_ROOT="C:\f3decomp\vs2005"
python C:\f3decomp\win_tools\msvc9.py /c /O2 /GS /EHsc /MT /Gy /FAs probe.cpp
```

Result (read the `.asm`): `this` -> **esi**, the parameter -> **ebp**, the zero -> **ebx** (still one
shared zero across all three Clears, and still `cmp eax,ebx` instead of ORIG's `test eax,eax`).
So when R1's statements are compiled almost alone, the parameter takes the *first* cross-call
register it can (ebp, because `this` took esi) and the zero still gets the leftover — allocation
follows definition order, and the zero never displaces the parameter. In the full function `this`
sits in ebp instead (it is live across the EH region), so `navMesh` takes the next register, esi.

Consequence: the colour is decided by which *other* web already owns esi/edi in ORIG — and the
comparison is the preferred-array base, which both versions put in `esi` at 0x022a. In ORIG `navMesh`
lives in ebx across the whole triangle loop and therefore cannot take esi; in ours its range stops at
0x0043 (before the loop), so esi is free for it and it takes it, forcing the reload at 0x00c6 and the
separate zeros. Making our `navMesh` web span the loop is the only thing that can flip it, and
nothing in the source survives the front-end to force that (the reload of a stack parameter is free,
so the optimizer always re-derives it).

## Correction to the brief's mechanism (important)

The brief says ORIG "holds a zero in ebx across the three Clears and keeps navMesh in ebx". Measured:

* ORIG: `mov ebx,[esp+0x44]` -> **`navMesh` owns `ebx`** and is read from `ebx` for the rest of the
  function (`[ebx+8]` flags, `[ebx+0x40]`, `[ebx+0x3c]`, ...). **ORIG has no shared zero register**:
  it materialises the zero separately in each Clear expansion — `xor esi,esi` in block 1 (because
  `eax` holds `pBuffer` and is live), `xor eax,eax` freshly in blocks 2 and 3, and `uiFlags = 0`
  is a plain immediate store (`mov dword ptr [ebp+8],0` at 0x00b0).
* Ours: `navMesh` gets `esi`, dies at 0x0043, and is **reloaded from `[esp+0x44]`** at 0x00c6 and
  again at 0x0151; the constant 0 gets `ebx` and is reused by all three Clears and by `uiFlags = 0`
  (`mov [ebp+8],ebx`). The `cmp reg,ebx` vs `test reg,reg` differences at 0x0056/0x0070/0x008f/0x00ae
  are not separate defects — they disappear for free if the constant loses `ebx`.

## The residue is offset shift plus colouring, not shape

Beyond the R1 colour, the R3 differences are **one block-placement difference**: ORIG lays out both
`Add` growth blocks at the end of the function (preferred array at 0x029b, connected array at 0x02c4),
ours inlines the preferred one right after the alloc-4 block (0x0257) and puts only the connected one
at the end (0x02c3). That alone shifts every later address by ~4 bytes (ORIG's `jne` at 0x022d is a
6-byte near jump, ours a 2-byte short jump at 0x022d) and produces most of the 39 lines counted in
0x200-0x2ff and 19 in 0x300-0x3ff. The instruction *sequences* on both sides of that shift are
identical. Both `Add` guards already match (`jne` on not-full, `jne` on capacity!=0), so this is the
compiler's hot/cold block ordering, not a source guard direction.

Per `compiler_tips.md`: webs are coloured highest-cost-first and each takes the lowest-score free
register; ties go `eax, ecx, edx, esi, edi, ebx, ebp`, so **`esi` beats `ebx` on a tie**. Ours gives
`navMesh` `esi`, which means our `navMesh` web is the highest-cost cross-call web. For ORIG to give it
`ebx`, `esi` and `edi` must already be taken (by the block-1 array base in `edi` and the block-1 zero
in `esi`, both live across the `_Deallocate` call). Reproducing that needs our zero web to *not* span
the three Clear calls, which no source spelling I tried achieves.
